# IT265 Module 2: One-Page Treatment

**Working game title:**

Write one coherent, one-page-length treatment. Use the four prompts to begin, then combine your answers into natural prose.

## Four-Sentence Core

1. **Premise:** Who is the player, what is the situation, and what problem matters?
2. **Experience and loop:** What should play feel like, and what does the player repeatedly decide or do?
3. **Goal and pressure:** What changes, what limits the player, and how does play end?
4. **Scope:** What makes this version distinctive, and what can you test first with affordable physical materials?

## Treatment Draft

In Deckrush: Blitz, the player is an adventurer traveling through four different sections while trying to reach the end of the adventure. Along the way, the player will run into enemies that they have to defeat using cards. The player collects new cards throughout the game and uses them to build their deck as they progress.
The game should feel similar to The Game of Life, where you roll dice to move through each section. The main gameplay loop is using your selected beginning cards to make it through all four sections and survive until the end. Players do this by defeating enemies and collecting the cards that they drop.
The main goal is to make it through all four sections and defeat the enemies along the way. As the player gets further into the game, they will collect more cards and have more options for how they play. The game ends when the player reaches the end of the fourth section.
The main idea that makes Deckrush: Blitz different is combining dice-based movement with card collecting, adventuring, and enemy encounters. The game is inspired by the progression of The Game of Life and the card-based gameplay of games like Slay the Spire and Hades. The first physical prototype can be made with paper cards, a simple board divided into four sections, dice, and basic enemy cards. This will let me test the main gameplay before adding more cards, enemies, and other features.
